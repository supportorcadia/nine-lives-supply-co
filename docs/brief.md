# Client brief — Nine Lives Supply Co.

We make outdoor gear for cats and we make it in Orkney. The reason we exist is that almost everything sold in a pet shop is designed for a cat that lives in a first-floor flat in Guildford. Ours live outside in horizontal rain, on single-track roads with no streetlights and no pavements, and they come home when they feel like it. The whole range is aimed at the four months of the year when it goes dark at half past three.

There are about twenty-five products in five colourways: Slate, Moss, Ember, Sand and Hi-Vis. Collars, engraved ID tags, tracker pouches, harnesses and leads, an insulated outdoor shelter, weatherproof feeding kit, carriers, travel mats and bowls, and a handful of toys for the indoor months. Prices run from £8 for a textured rubber ball to £105 for the backpack carrier with the dome window, with the Bothy Shelter at £96 just behind it. Three kits bundle the obvious combinations at a saving. Harnesses come in four sizes. Everything else is one size and a colour choice.

Two things make buying from us more awkward than buying a jumper. The first is that nobody knows what size their cat is. A cat is not a dog, you cannot ask it to stand still with a tape measure round its ribs, and the difference between a harness that holds and one the cat reverses straight out of in a vet car park is about two centimetres of girth. The second is the engraving. Every brass and aluminium tag is cut to order with a phone number on it, which adds days before the parcel goes anywhere. People do not expect that and then they ring up about it.

Everything ships from Kirkwall. We sell to the UK only and in pounds only. We are straight with people that island and Highland deliveries take longer than the mainland, because we would rather say it on the page than argue about it afterwards. A small amount goes to two vets and a farm supply shop on the islands, but that is a sideline and the site is for the public.

What we want out of the site: fewer returns, which at the moment are almost entirely harnesses in the wrong size, and more people buying the tag at the same time as the collar instead of coming back three weeks later once the cat has already gone missing once. Hi-Vis outsells the other four colourways combined and we would like to keep it that way. We also want somebody who has never once thought a cat needs any of this to understand in about four seconds why it might. Our honest view is that a size guide is not really necessary, because most people know their own cat.

On taste. Finisterre (https://finisterre.com/) is the one we keep coming back to. It is a cold-water brand that never shows you a beach in sunshine, the photography looks like the weather it was actually taken in, and the clothes are shot as objects rather than on somebody smiling. That is exactly right for us. Alpkit (https://alpkit.com/) we like for being plain about what a thing is for and what it is made of. Highland Park (https://www.highlandparkwhisky.com/) manage to do Orkney without a standing stone or a puffin anywhere in sight, which we respect, because we are not putting a puffin on anything. Snow Peak (https://www.snowpeak.com/) and Topo Designs (https://topodesigns.com/) both use flat blocks of colour in a way we like, although Topo is a good deal brighter than we are.

We cannot stand the big pet retailers. Pets at Home (https://www.petsathome.com/), Chewy (https://www.chewy.com/), Zooplus (https://www.zooplus.co.uk/). Cartoon lettering, a discount sticker on everything, a kitten in a teacup. They make the product look like a toy. We want ours to look like something you would buy in a chandlery. The site should feel dark and heavy, like a winter night here. We would also like everything we sell visible on one page so nobody has to click around to find it.

What already exists: a wordmark in a heavy grotesque, drawn for us; a stencil cut of the same mark that goes on the shelter and the carriers; a colour reference sheet for the five colourways; the packaging, which is a plain kraft box with the stencil on the lid and one hi-vis sticker; and a swing tag with the girth measuring instructions printed on the back. There is also a photography archive from three winters of shoots across the islands. None of it will be supplied.
