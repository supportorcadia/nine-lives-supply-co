# Decisions — Nine Lives Supply Co.

Design judgments only. Each entry: what was chosen, one line of why.

- **Skipped the Ship Studio onboarding questionnaire.** The client brief answers every onboarding question; SITE.md is written from it at the end of the build instead.
- **One long manifest page instead of category pages.** The brief asks for everything visible on one page; Real Seeds and chandlery catalogues prove a fixed index plus numbered entries carries 25 items without a menu.
- **Girth before size on every harness.** The client says a size guide isn't needed; the returns figure and the forum language ("still big on him", "two fingers") say sizing is the entire problem. Girth entry replaces the size dropdown as the first control.
- **Tag as a line item on the collar.** The brief's second goal is buying the tag with the collar; offering it inline with the phone number field removes the separate trip.
- **Hi-Vis preselected everywhere.** The brief wants it to keep outselling the others; default beats persuasion.
- **Dark ground.** Every reference in the category is white; the brief asks for a winter night; the product is reflective and reads best against dark. Earned, not aesthetic.
- **Rows 10 (Bothy Shelter) and 13 (Noup Carrier) get a wide 4:3 image; everything else is square.** The two biggest objects and the two highest prices; breaking the row rhythm twice tells the eye where the money is.
- **Three night-road environmental images interrupt the manifest (opening, before Out there, after Indoor months).** They carry the "four seconds" argument; the product shots carry the sale.
- **Basket holds its render until saved items are read.** Otherwise the page flashes "Nothing in it" before the real basket appears.
- **Styles live in app/site.css, imported from globals.css.** The Ship Studio dev server's CSS worker stuck on the original globals.css; a new module compiled cleanly. Not a design decision, recorded so nobody merges the two files back and hits the same wall.
- **Check-in 2 correction: offer first, cats in frame, warmer palette.** The client found the cut stark and unclear. The opening now states what is sold and for whom; an "Is this for you?" panel names the four problems (seen, found, held, dry) with item numbers; every product carries a plain kind line under its ownable name; the imagery DNA admits real cats and lamplight; the ground shifts from blue-black to peat. The manifest, girth-first and tag-on-collar structure is unchanged.
- **Homepage features six products; the full manifest moves to /gear.** The client asked for a featured selection rather than a one-page site. The manifest structure survives intact on the shop page; the homepage now makes the argument (offer, girth, tag-with-collar) and the shop makes the sale.
- **Featured six: 01 collar, 06 harness (large, shown on a cat), 03 tag, 05 pouch, 10 shelter, 23 kit.** One from each problem (seen, found, held, dry) plus the kit that bundles the first three.
- **Palette moved from dark to warm oat and kraft; type moved to Fraunces and Source Serif 4.** The client found the dark cut cold and asked for warmth and a serif. Kraft is the packaging colour, so it is still the brand's own material. Peat night is kept for the footer and for the photography's night outside.
- **Copy warmed: cats are "they", the owners are "cat people", and the reasons say so.** The client said they love their cats and wanted that heard. Specifics stayed; sentiment was added in the reasons, not in adjectives.
- **Phase 7 (style) applied before Phase 6 (imagery).** The client was reacting to grey boxes as coldness; showing colour and type first made the feedback loop useful.
- **Full-screen video hero, copy bottom-left, buttons bottom-right.** The client asked for it. The video file goes at public/media/hero.mp4 with a poster at public/media/hero-poster.jpg; until they exist the slot shows a labelled frame. Reduced-motion users get the poster only. The offer panel now sits directly under the hero so the first scroll answers "is this for me?".
- **Imagery run scoped to 56 images.** Hero poster, 8 cat/environment shots, 25 Hi-Vis object shots, 6 featured details, and colourway variants for the four hero products. The full set (all colourway variants and details, ~190 images) has prompts ready in docs/image-plan.md and is a second run, not a design question.
- **Signature move is the tape rule pulled out on scroll.** It animates the repeating detail rather than adding a new one, and it comes from the product's own physical truth (a tape round a chest). Nothing on the page moves faster than the reader scrolls.
- **Frames show the real photograph when the file exists and a labelled kraft box otherwise.** Lets the site build up as the batch lands without code changes, and makes any missing image obvious in the audit.
- **Theme returned to dark, kept warm.** The client's reasoning: safety gear for the dark should sit in the dark. Peat ground, cream text, Hi-Vis as the action colour. The serif type, warm copy and lamplit photography stay, so it reads as a lit room on a winter night rather than a car park.
- **Archivo for headlines, numbers and buttons; Newsreader for body; wordmark in Hi-Vis top and bottom.** The client pointed out the grotesque wordmark sat oddly over serif headings and the footer mark was Hi-Vis while the header's wasn't. One display family in the wordmark's voice, one warm serif for reading.
- **The offer panel became four photo tiles: Seen, Found, Held, Dry.** The client found the text version a wall of words. Each job gets a photograph already in the set, the word in the wordmark's face with a Hi-Vis full stop, and one line. Same four links, now something to look at.
- **Consistency check, first catch: the shelter environment shot showed a different object from the shelter product shot.** The client spotted it. Regenerated with the product shot as reference; the rule from the art direction stands: a product appearing in more than one image must be the same object.
- **Process shots added for the about page.** They were never in the queue; the page had three empty frames. Same lamp, same window, same bench across all three so they read as one room.
- **Product pages hide empty detail slots and fall back to the Hi-Vis shot for ungenerated colourways.** No labelled frame is visible anywhere on the finished site; the remaining variants are a second run, prompts ready.
- **The apostrophe lint rule is switched off.** The copy is full of contractions; escaping every one as an HTML entity would make the pages unreadable to the client.
- **Homepage band rebuilt as "Sewn, engraved and stencilled in Orkney" with the three workshop shots.** The audit named the old band as the one section that was only there because sites have one; it now shows the making, which is the brief's own proof.
- **Favicon is the 9 alone, Hi-Vis on peat.** The art direction said so; the starter's placeholder was still in place.
- **The Seen / Found / Held / Dry panel sits on kraft with peat text; homepage sections get double the space.** The client found the panel invisible on peat and the page crowded. Kraft is the box colour, so the one light block on the site is the packaging.
- **The "2 cm" figure now reads as a sentence: "2 cm is the difference between a harness that holds and one a cat reverses straight out of."** The client asked what the number meant; a figure without its noun is decoration.
- **Girth tape image reshot with plain ticks and no digits.** Generated digits were out of sequence and the client saw it; a tape with no numbers cannot be wrong.
- **Harness sizes rebased to 35–56 cm (1: 35–40, 2: 40–45, 3: 45–50, 4: 50–56).** The client says a normal healthy young cat is 40–50 cm round the chest. The first table (26–43) came from a competitor's published chart in the research, which measures differently; the client's number wins because it is their cats and their returns.
- **Downloads from two ChatGPT tabs at once collided.** A product image landed as the girth photo for a few minutes. Recovered; from now on any image saved by hand is fetched from the page under its own filename, never by "newest download".
- **Menu says "Fit" and "Delivery & Returns", not "Girth" and "From Kirkwall".** The client didn't like the titles. "Girth" is the site's word once you're inside; a stranger scanning a menu needs the plain job. The page addresses stay the same so nothing breaks.
- **The mark is a 9 with a cat's tail.** The client asked for something cat-themed but very subtle. No ears, no whiskers, no silhouette: the tail of the numeral curls back on itself the way a sitting cat's does, and that is the whole idea. Stroke-drawn so it holds at 22 px and 72 px. Footer version is stencil-cut with two bridges, because the brief said the footer mark is the stencil on the box.
- **Tape rule doubled: 18 px, three tick heights, lighter twine.** The client found it too small to read as a tape. The 80 px major tick is now legible at a glance and the scroll pull-out has something to pull.
- **Tape cat reshot a second time.** Digits came back and the tape had a metal end tab. New prompt: ticks only, both ends out of frame behind the cat. Nothing left in the image that can be wrong.
- **Hero video made with Remotion, from the photographs.** The client asked for Remotion and has no footage yet. Four night shots, four seconds each, drift and crossfade, the tape rule pulling out along the bottom; the loop opens and closes on the doorstep so it seams. When real winter footage exists, the file is swapped and nothing else changes.
- **The mark got a head and ears.** The tail alone didn't read as a cat to the client. The bowl of the 9 is now the head with two ears rising off it; still one colour, still stroke-drawn, still a 9 first.
- **The track shot in the hero film is reframed, not dropped.** The cat sat bottom-centre, under the headline. Each shot in the film can now be zoomed about a fixed point; this one is pushed in so the cat walks to the right of the text.
- **The hero film's last crossfade now happens inside the film.** The shelter shot used to fade out after the film had already ended, so the loop cut hard back to the doorstep. Every shot now fades out inside its own four seconds, and the first frame and the last frame are the same picture.
- **Made in Orkney no longer ends with its own tape rule.** The footer opens with one, so the page had two stacked at the bottom. The page-end rule is removed; no other page had the same doubling.
- **Colourway variants are recolours of the Hi-Vis master, not new photographs.** The client saw that the four Ware Feeding Station variants were four different objects. Done first for 11: the Hi-Vis shot is uploaded as the reference and only the powder coat changes, with a Hi-Vis strip kept on the lid edge. The same method is available for every other colourway product.
- **Bothy Shelter colourways recoloured from the Hi-Vis master; product and shop images shown at their own shape.** The main product image was forced to 4:5 and cut the sides off every square shot and the three wide ones (10, 13, 25). It now takes the photo's own proportions. Shop rows change picture with the swatch, as the product page already did.
- **All 23 colourway products now use recolours of their Hi-Vis master.** The client found the backpack and Geo Carrier variants were different designs too, so the whole range was done in one pass: 84 images, one candidate each, checked on contact sheets against the originals. Every variant is the same object, hardware, position and doorstep; only the coloured material changes. The previous variants are kept outside the project in case any are wanted back.
- **Headless Shopify, provisioned through Vercel.** The client wanted the store to actually work and asked for Shopify. Vercel's marketplace integration created a fresh sandbox store (nothing existing touched, on the client's instruction) and put the Storefront credentials on the project. Shopify owns prices, stock, cart, checkout, payment and orders; the site keeps its own copy and photographs and marries them by handle. The client's account had to claim the sandbox before the Shopify CLI could log in.
- **Colourway and Size are Shopify options; girth and engraving are line attributes.** Options are what Shopify prices and stocks. Girth and the two engraving lines are per-order text, so they ride on the cart line and show up in checkout and on the order, which is where the person cutting the tag needs them.
- **Products are loaded from the catalogue by a script, not typed into Shopify.** `scripts/shopify-seed.mjs` uses productSet through the Shopify CLI so the 25 products, 162 variants and 121 images can be replaced in one run whenever the catalogue changes.
- **The fake order confirmation page is gone.** Shopify's checkout shows the real one.
- **First test order placed through the whole chain, 17 Sep 2026.** Order #1001 in Shopify: Moss Skerry Collar and an engraved brass tag, both engraving lines on the line item, Posted from Kirkwall at £3.95, £35.95 total, paid through the test gateway. The storefront password (Online Store → Preferences) gates checkout until a plan is chosen; that goes away with the plan.
- **Checkout styled to the site through Shopify's checkout editor, not the API.** The Checkout Branding API and its newer configuration API both refuse changes on the trial plan. The editor in the admin does allow logo, palette, section colours, Shopify-library fonts and transparent inputs, so that's what carries the brand: peat, Hi-Vis, Archivo, Newsreader, the lock-up. Corner radius is already square by default. Custom font files and radius settings are Plus-only; uploading the site's own woff2 files was refused on trial. The tape band was rendered but there's no header banner slot on this plan.
- **Portfolio pass, 18 Sep 2026.** The site is for showing and testing, not trading, so the tester note in the basket gives the demo password and test card rather than hiding them. Link-preview metadata added because the address will be sent around. Small homepage cards restack at phone width (name and number on one line, price beneath) instead of breaking names a word per line. The phone menu says "Delivery" so the basket count fits. Frames read an image manifest instead of probing for optional photographs, which removes the 404s that filled the console on 18 product pages.
