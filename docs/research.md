# Research — Nine Lives Supply Co.

One pass, four questions. Fetched 15 September 2026. Highland Park, Force 4 and Marine Superstore blocked automated fetching (403); Highland Park covered via trade press on its October 2024 rebrand.

## 1. Reference forensics

### Liked

**Finisterre** (finisterre.com)
- Type: one bold grotesque sans throughout; headlines set uppercase ("MADE FOR LIFE WHERE LAND ENDS"), body moderate weight. Product tiles: name, price, occasional badge, no description.
- Nav: Men / Women / Impact / Stories / Lived & Loved. Mega-menu pairs campaign photo with link lists.
- Order: hero → new-in → gendered shop blocks → collaboration feature → values → journal → product carousels.
- Photography: clothing on model, accessories flat; bright neutral light, plain or faintly textured backdrop; ~square crops in tiles. Hero photography is weather-real (grey sea, wet rock, overcast).
- Colour: interface white/neutral; colour lives only in product and hero photos (navy, ochre, earth).
- Density: 2–4 tiles per row, image-led, almost no text in grid.
- Scroll: horizontal carousels with arrows; nothing scroll-driven.

**Alpkit** (alpkit.com)
- Type: bold uppercase sans nav (Men, Women, Camping, Equipment, Bike, Outlet, Explore); large display headings; compact utilitarian body.
- Nav: seven categories with multi-level dropdowns; activity-based entry tiles (Camping, Hiking, Trail Running…).
- Order: hero carousel → activity tiles → bikes → stores → repair → newsletter → principles.
- Photography: landscape hero with atmosphere; product tiles consistent crop, environmental context.
- Colour: black text on white; brand colour only on CTAs and logo.
- Copy: use-case first — "From fast hikes and mountain days to everyday trail miles." Plain what-it's-for sentences under every product.

**Highland Park** (via The Grocer / Spirits Business, Oct 2024 rebrand)
- Dropped Norse/Viking symbolism entirely. Orkney signalled by: heather-flecked labels, wood-grain from casks, a "product of Orkney" mark cut into the glass, a bright cold palette described as "Orkney's ethereal light and invigorating climate", and portraits of actual Orkney residents.
- Lesson: place is carried by material, light and people, not by landmarks.

**Snow Peak** (snowpeak.com)
- Type: uppercase sans category headers, large bold hero type, minimal body.
- Order: hero → 8-cell feature grid (Tents, Takibi, Furniture, IGT, Stoves, Cookware, Drinkware, Tableware) → collection cards → experience blocks → about → Instagram.
- Colour: whites, greys, wood; flat colour blocks carry section headers.
- Density: deliberately low, one item per tile, "View All" links out.

**Topo Designs** (topodesigns.com)
- Type: clean sans; medium-weight product names, smaller descriptive line, regular-weight prices.
- Order: hero → new arrivals carousel → category grids (4 columns).
- Flat colour blocks anchor messaging areas; product cards on white with photo dominant. Mixed overhead flat-lay and on-model. Brighter than the brief wants.

### Disliked

**Pets at Home** — six-cell species grid, mega-menus with 30+ links per category, promo hero ("Fetch great autumn finds", "50% off your first month's flea, tick and worm plan"), advice carousel, product carousels.
**Chewy** — promo carousel, "Who are you shopping for?" cards, tiles carry image + title + star rating + review count + price + badge + Add to Cart; "Deal"/"Promo" labels; cream/grey with orange and teal.
**Zooplus** — rotating banner offers, "15% off…", "30% off…", 50+ brand-logo slider, 16–20 links per dropdown, green accent on white.

### What liked and disliked have in common
- All eight are white-interface sites. Nobody in either camp is dark.
- All use a hero-then-carousel homepage. The liked ones use fewer, larger tiles; the disliked ones cram badges and ratings into the tile.
- All use category mega-menus. The liked sites split by person/activity; disliked by species.
- All show price as a plain number; none of the liked sites show a strike-through.
- Real difference is in tile contents (image + name + price vs image + rating + badge + button) and in photography (weather-real objects vs bright lifestyle with animal).

## 2. Customer language (verbatim)

Harness escape (TheCatSite threads 275676, 407913, 473058; Supakit reviews):
- "he escaped his harness and ran away"
- "So Jackie has been escaping his harness lately. He escaped 3 times."
- "he wears a size small but it is still big on him."
- "it is way too loose. She's still in the largest size of small pet harness"
- "If I tighten it up tight around his neck it makes it harder for him to escape but it seems uncomfortable."
- "fitted with room for 2 loose fingers between him and the straps"
- "he is a daemon wriggler and never managed to get out of it YET"
- "I don't think I would feel happy taking any of my cats out again using a harness one had managed to get out from"
- "I was totally frantic about it when it happened"
- "they can't Houdini their way out of it"
- "She has literally never slipped it"
- Mechanism as described by a poster: "When a cat backs up, the leash pulls the belly strap forward, the cat tucks her elbows inside that strap, it slides up the torso, and she walks out of the neck loop."
- Sizing advice across sources: "measure, not weigh"; "two cats at the same weight can differ substantially in neck and chest girth"; "if between sizes, go smaller for a snug fit".

Collars, tags, visibility (TheCatSite thread 440653; RSPCA; Animal Friends):
- "make a mostly black escape artist easier to see"
- "He really scared us when he darted out the front door and into the street last night!"
- "It's really hard to see him to catch him if he darts out the door at night, though, because he's mostly black"
- "Microchipped in case he gets out and a collar with a tag"
- "quick-release breakaway clasps" so cats "can get free if snagged on a fence or branch"
- Vocabulary: breakaway, quick-release, snagged, reflective, bell, microchipped, "gets out", "darts out".

Engraving (Cheshire & Wain, Pet-Tags, Pet ID Tags Express):
- Competitors state lead time in Shipping & Returns accordion: "Please allow an additional 10 working days for our engraving service."
- Fast movers say "processes and ships orders within 24 hours" and "2–3 working days".
- Engraving captured as "Engraving Front:" / "Engraving Back:" fields, "1 line of text per side".
- Buyer phrasing: "name and phone number", "two lines of text".

Shelters (Home & Roost, Yum Yum Mama):
- "Do not use hay as this absorbs moisture, as do towels and fabric blankets" — straw vs blankets is the argument.
- "Overheating is a concern if two or more cats will be using the shelter"; "turf wars".
- "raised off the ground so cold and damp can't seep up from below"
- "keeps your cat dry and cosy without needing assembly"
- Whether the cat will use it at all: "a catnip stick that will gently lure them in".

What goes wrong, in their words: the cat backs out, the collar snaps off and is lost, the tag arrives after the cat has already gone missing, the shelter gets damp, another cat moves in.

## 3. Category landscape

**Default pattern (every competitor):** white ground, species mega-menu, promo hero carousel, grid tiles with rating stars and badges, size handled as a dropdown with a size-chart link, engraving handled as two text inputs and a lead-time line buried in the shipping accordion. Cat shown in frame, usually indoors, usually smiling owner.

**Structurally different:**
- **Supakit** — puts measuring instructions mid-page on the product page with two diagrams (chest "around the front of their breastbone", belly "snugly behind their elbows") and a video, with an explicit between-sizes rule. Still a dropdown, but sizing is on the page rather than a link.
- **Cheshire & Wain** — leans on materials (brass, leather, gold plate) and jeweller-style photography on black; treats the cat product as an object. Closest thing in the category to "chandlery".
- **Home & Roost** — sells shelters through long-form advice ("how to choose") rather than the product grid; the argument sells the product.

## 4. Out-of-category structure

1. **Labour and Wait** (labourandwait.co.uk) — ironmonger's grid: name, price, nothing else. "Knife Block." "Fisherman's Smock Olive." Objects photographed flat on neutral. Shows how little text a tile needs when the object is honest.
2. **Real Seed Catalogue** (realseeds.co.uk) — the whole range down one long page with a left index, sowing-time squares as a repeating device ("Dark green squares show the main sowing times"), instructions written by named staff. Proves an entire catalogue on one page works when the index is fixed and the entries are dense.
3. **Hiut Denim** (hiutdenim.co.uk) — small catalogue entered by *fit* (Barrel, Tapered, Slim, Straight, Wide) not by product; "Sent from Aberteifi" as a place-of-dispatch line. Fit as the front door; dispatch place as a brand line.
4. **Farrow & Ball colour card** (farrow-ball.com/paint-colours) — every colour named and numbered ("Hog Plum No. CB1"), swatch first, 132 shades in a fixed grid. A colourway system as the organising principle.
5. **Chandlery catalogues** (Force 4 / Marine Superstore, blocked to fetch; known pattern) — rope sold by diameter and breaking strain, shackles by pin size, everything in a dense table with a line drawing. Spec-first, no lifestyle, no persuasion. The brief literally asks for this.

## Five findings most likely to change what gets built

1. **Nobody in the category is dark.** All eight references, liked and disliked, are white sites. A dark, heavy site is not a reskin of anything the client sees; it is structurally the odd one out, and the product photography has to be planned for it from the start (objects lit against dark, not cut-outs on white).
2. **Sizing is the whole returns problem and the client thinks it isn't.** Buyer language is entirely about girth, "still big on him", "two fingers", and the reverse-out mechanic. The site has to make girth the first question on the harness, before the size dropdown, without calling it a "size guide".
3. **Engraving lead time is buried everywhere.** Competitors put it in the shipping accordion and get phone calls; the client gets the same calls. The lead time has to sit next to the engraving field and again in the cart.
4. **Whole-catalogue-on-one-page is a solved problem outside the category** (Real Seeds, chandlery catalogues, colour cards). A fixed index plus dense numbered entries works; a carousel-per-category homepage does not.
5. **The five colourways are a system, not a filter.** Farrow & Ball and Topo/Snow Peak show flat colour blocks doing structural work. Hi-Vis is the one that sells; treat it as the default in every block and the others as the alternatives, not five equal swatches.
