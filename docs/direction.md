# Direction — Nine Lives Supply Co.

## The choice

The shop is one long page (/gear) that lists everything the company makes, numbered 01 to 25, in the order it matters on a winter night: what is on the cat, what says whose it is, what it comes home to, what it travels in, what it does when it stays in. The page is built around the two things that actually go wrong: the harness comes back because it was the wrong girth, and the tag gets bought three weeks after the collar. Girth is therefore the first control on every harness, before size, and the tag is a line item on the collar rather than a separate trip. The obvious site we are not building is the outdoor-brand template with a dark hero, a category mega-menu, and a carousel per collection. Every reference in the brief, liked and disliked, is that site on white. This one is a chandlery catalogue for cats, set at night.

## Premise

**Outdoor gear for cats that go out in the dark, and come home. Everything we make, on one page.**

*Amended twice after check-in 2. Second amendment: the client does not want a one-page site. The homepage now opens with the offer and features six products; the full numbered manifest lives at /gear. First amendment: the client found the first cut stark and unclear about the offer. The structure stands; the opening now states the offer in plain words, cats appear in the photography, and the palette warms from blue-black to peat with lamplight. The night is somewhere the cat comes home to, not somewhere it is lost in.*

## The bet

A visitor who has never thought a cat needs gear reads the first line ("It goes dark at half past three and the cat is still out"), sees a hi-vis collar lit in headlights, and gets it. A visitor who already owns a cat scrolls a numbered list, finds the harness, types a girth, gets a size, adds the tag with the number already filled in, and is told before paying that the engraving adds three working days. Returns fall because girth is asked before size. Tags attach because they are offered on the collar, not in the menu.

## Navigation

- No mega-menu. A fixed index (left rail on desktop, a single row above the content on mobile) lists the five sections with their item numbers: **01–09 On the cat · 10–12 Out there · 13–17 In transit · 18–22 Indoor months · 23–25 Kits.** Clicking scrolls. The index tracks position.
- Top bar: wordmark left; **Girth**, **From Kirkwall** (shipping and returns), **Made in Orkney** (about), and the basket right. Four words, nothing else.
- Every product has its own page at /gear/[slug] for detail, but nothing requires visiting it; you can buy from the manifest.

## Entering the catalogue

The homepage states the offer, answers "is this for you?", features the six things most cats leave the house in, makes the girth argument, and hands over to /gear. The shop page is the catalogue. Below a short, heavy opening statement (not a full-viewport hero) the manifest begins with item 01. Each entry is a numbered row: number, photograph, name, one line of what it is for, materials, price, colourway blocks with Hi-Vis first and preselected, and an add control. Harness rows carry the girth control inline. Collar rows carry the "add the tag" line inline with a field for the phone number. Kits show their contents by item number.

## Product page composition

Two columns, unequal. Left, roughly 55%: the photograph stack, object first, then detail, then one in-weather shot. Right: the chandlery spec block — number and name, price, one paragraph of what it is for, then a table (material, closure, weight, girth range or dimensions, reflectivity, made in) and the controls in the fixed order: colourway (Hi-Vis first), girth then size if a harness, engraving text and lead time if a tag, quantity, add. Below both columns: "goes with", listed by item number, and the shipping line in the owner's words.

## How much is on screen

Desktop: the index rail plus two or three manifest rows. Rows are tall enough that the photograph reads as an object, not a thumbnail. Mobile: one row per screen, photograph above, controls below. The page is long and is meant to be.

## Typefaces

- **Archivo** (variable, self-hosted). Weight 800–900 for headings and the manifest numbers; the width axis at 125% for the wordmark so it reads as the heavy grotesque the brief describes.
- **Instrument Sans** (variable, self-hosted) for body, spec tables and controls, with tabular figures on.

## Palette

- Peat `#17130F` — the ground. Panel `#231D17` — rows and cards. Rule `#3A3229`.
- Cream `#F2EAD9` — text. Cream dim `#A89F8E` — secondary.
- Lamp `#E9A94F` — warmth, used in photography (lit windows, porch lights) and for the one "goes with" link colour.
- **Hi-Vis `#D7FF3F`** — the one accent. Used for the add control, the active index item, the selected colourway, the girth readout, and nothing else.
- Colourway blocks, used only as swatches and never as interface colour: Slate `#5C6670`, Moss `#4A5A3E`, Ember `#B8481F`, Sand `#C7B48E`, Hi-Vis `#D7FF3F`.

## Homepage copy, in this voice

Outdoor gear for cats that go out after dark. Collars you can see from a car, tags with your number on, harnesses that hold, and a shelter that stays dry. Made in Orkney for cats that live like ours, and posted from Kirkwall to anywhere in the UK.

## Template test

Swap the logo and palette onto a bike shop: the numbered one-page manifest would survive, but the girth control, the tag riding on the collar, the section order taken from a night outdoors, the Hi-Vis-first colourway rule and the Kirkwall shipping line would all be meaningless. The structure only works for a business whose returns are a sizing problem and whose second purchase is a safety item. Passes.
